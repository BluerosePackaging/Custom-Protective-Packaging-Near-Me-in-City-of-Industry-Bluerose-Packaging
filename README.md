## How Custom Protective Packaging Helps Your Operation

### Keep Products in Position

Movement inside a carton can cause items to rub against one another or strike the box walls. Properly fitted inserts and dividers help limit unwanted movement and keep products positioned during routine handling.

### Protect Delicate Surfaces

Finished metal parts, painted components, electronics, and decorative products may be vulnerable to scratches or surface marks. Suitable foam or other protective layers can separate these surfaces from surrounding materials.

### Provide Support for Unusual Shapes

Products with protruding sections, uneven edges, or irregular dimensions can be difficult to pack using standard cushioning alone. Custom-cut pieces and shaped supports can provide coverage where the product needs it most.

### Make Packing More Consistent

When employees repeatedly pack the same product, a defined arrangement of inserts and cushioning helps reduce guesswork. It can also make it easier to check that each shipment contains the necessary protective materials before the carton is closed.

A well-planned insert can position a product without relying on excessive loose fill. Depending on the design, this may help organize multiple components within one carton and reduce unnecessary gaps.

### Support Safer Storage and Distribution

Products may be stacked, moved between departments, or prepared for delivery at different times. Suitable internal supports and an appropriately selected outer package help keep the contents organized throughout these activities.

Different industries handle different product types, so the material and packaging design should reflect the item being shipped.

* **Electronics and components:** Foam inserts and dividers can help position circuit boards, connectors, and electronic assemblies. Products with ESD requirements need packaging with appropriate static-control properties.
* **Industrial and manufacturing parts:** Protective blocks, separators, and fitted cushioning can help keep machine components, tools, and replacement parts organized.
* **Automotive components:** Foam layers and custom supports may help protect selected trim pieces, fittings, and finished parts against contact damage.
* **Medical and laboratory equipment:** Suitable inserts can help stabilize instruments and accessories during movement. Any special cleanliness, sterility, or regulatory requirements must be addressed separately.
* **Furniture and home goods:** Protective sheets, edge guards, and cushioning can help separate panels, finished surfaces, and smaller components.
* **Retail and e-commerce products:** Fitted inserts can keep products organized inside shipping cartons and improve the presentation when the package is opened.
* **Glass and fragile merchandise:** Dividers and cushioning can help separate individual items, provided the complete package is designed for the product's fragility and shipping conditions.

## What to Consider Before Choosing a Packaging Solution

A successful packaging design begins with understanding the product and how it will travel. Before selecting materials, review these important details.

**Product dimensions and shape:** Measure the item and identify exposed corners, delicate surfaces, and sections that need additional support.

**Weight and load distribution:** Heavy products may need stronger supports or a more robust outer carton than lightweight items of similar size.

**Handling and shipping conditions:** Consider how often the package will be moved, whether it will be stacked, and the types of transportation involved.

**Cushioning requirements:** Different products need different levels of impact absorption and separation. Choose materials that suit the item's sensitivity and weight.

**Packaging quantity and frequency:** Recurring shipments may benefit from a repeatable insert design that makes packing easier to standardize.

**Storage and disposal considerations:** Review how packaging materials will be stored before use and whether recycling or material separation is important to your operation.

Taking these factors into account can help you select packaging based on actual product requirements rather than simply adding more material.

No. Some products benefit from foam, while others may need corrugated partitions, paper-based cushioning, protective wraps, or a combination of materials. The selection depends on the risks involved.

Yes. Properly sized inserts and dividers can help hold products in position and reduce unwanted movement. The design should account for the product's shape, weight, and expected handling conditions.
